The three tiers of profit

Revenue (the "top line") does not belong entirely to the owners. As goods are made, sold, and supported, costs peel away at three distinct stages: [1]

TierFormulaWhat it measuresControlling departments
1. Gross Profit
Margin: Gross Profit ÷ Revenue		 Revenue − COGS Product efficiency: Profit left after paying the direct costs of producing desks (lumber, hardware, factory labor). Procurement, Operations, Product R&D: material purchase prices, manufacturing waste, assembly speed.
2. Operating Profit (EBIT)
Margin: Operating Profit ÷ Revenue		 Gross Profit − Operating Expenses Commercial viability: Profit left after paying all ongoing operating overhead (salaries, marketing, rent, software). Sales, Marketing, HR, IT, Customer Service, Admin, Legal: headcounts, commissions, ad spend, cloud fees.
3. Net Profit
Margin: Net Profit ÷ Revenue		 Operating Profit − Interest − Taxes The bottom line: The ultimate residual profit available to reinvest in growth or distribute to shareholders. Finance & Executive Leadership: debt structure, interest rates, capital allocation, tax compliance.

Margins vs. Dollars: The Agent Optimization Trap

Autonomous agents must navigate a fundamental tension between percentages and absolute dollars:

The Percentage Illusion:
  • Suppose Cedar sells custom desks with a 50% Gross Margin ($1,000 price, $500 COGS = $500 gross profit per desk).
  • A corporate client offers to buy 100 standard desks in bulk, but demands a volume discount: $800 price, $500 COGS = $300 profit per desk (a lower 37.5% Gross Margin).
  • An AI agent rewarded solely on Gross Margin Percentage would reject the deal.
  • Yet the deal generates $30,000 in cash profit to cover overhead! Optimizing percentage margin in isolation destroys total earnings.

Agent-world guardrails for margins

  1. Multi-tier visibility: An agent optimizing Marketing or Sales spend must observe whether incremental revenue covers both direct COGS and operational overhead.
  2. Contribution margin test: Before approving a customer discount, verify: Discounted Price > Direct Variable COGS. If price falls below variable cost, each sale loses money.
  3. Beware false productivity: An HR or IT agent that indiscriminately cuts training or software tools improves this quarter's operating margin, but damages employee retention and customer satisfaction next quarter.

Your turn: evaluate a marketing campaign

Tell your teacher: Cedar has monthly revenue of $100,000.
  • Cost of Goods Sold (COGS) is $60,000.
  • Operating Expenses (SG&A across Sales, Marketing, HR, IT, Admin) are $25,000.
  • Bank loan interest is $3,000; corporate taxes are $2,400.
  1. Calculate Cedar's Gross Profit, Operating Profit, and Net Profit in dollars.
  2. Calculate Cedar's Gross Margin %, Operating Margin %, and Net Margin %.
  3. An automated Marketing agent proposes a $5,000 advertising campaign that generates $10,000 in new sales. Assuming the same 60% COGS rate and no other expense changes, how much does Cedar's Operating Profit change in dollars? Does Operating Margin % go up or down?
Ask the agent anything unclear about income statement categories, margin formulas, or departmental cost allocations.
