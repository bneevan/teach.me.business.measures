Business worlds for AI agents / Lesson 01 / 5 minutes

A sale is not cash.

Your win: explain how a company can make a profit while its bank balance falls.

Meet Cedar Desk Co.

Our fictional company designs, makes, and sells desks to businesses. Later, we’ll follow its eleven departments. First, we need three company-wide measures so departmental goals have context.

MeasureQuestion it answers
RevenueHow much sales income did we earn during this period?
ProfitHow much income remained after the period’s expenses?
Cash balanceHow much cash do we hold right now?

A customer can receive desks now and pay later. For this simple sale, we recognize revenue when control of the desks transfers to the customer, not when payment arrives. [1] Profit and cash flow therefore need not match; formal cash-flow reporting accounts for timing and non-cash differences. [2]

One month, two different stories

Cedar starts April with $10,000 in bank cash. It delivers 100 desks for $100 each. The customer receives control in April and pays the entire invoice in May.

All expenses for April total $7,000, including the cost of the desks sold and other operating expenses. All $7,000 is paid in April. Assume no other income, transactions, taxes, interest, opening inventory, or unpaid bills. These deliberately simple assumptions are not general accounting rules.

April profit = $10,000 revenue − $7,000 expenses = $3,000.
April closing cash = $10,000 opening cash + $0 received − $7,000 paid = $3,000.
April net cash flow = $0 − $7,000 = −$7,000.

The company earned a profit, but cash fell. The customer still owes $10,000—a receivable. The two $3,000 figures happen to match; profit and closing cash are not the same measure.

Why this matters for your agent world

In this scenario, Sales arranged a pay-later sale, Operations delivered the desks, and Finance tracks the invoice and collection. A Sales agent rewarded only for revenue could prefer more such deals even when cash is scarce. That is a possible incentive problem, not proof that credit sales are bad.

Modeling implication: represent delivery and payment as separate events. A proposed guardrail is to check projected cash against upcoming obligations before offering longer payment terms. We’ll learn how to choose such guardrails later.
