Business worlds for AI agents / Lesson 04 / 6 minutes

Follow the cash,
not just the sale.

Your win: build a dated cash timeline and locate a gap between paying suppliers and collecting customers.

Quick reference: Cash timeline ↗

Recall before we add anything

A cash purchase of qualifying current inventory leaves current liabilities unchanged. What happens to current ratio?

One batch of desks, four different events

Use a fresh, isolated Cedar example, not the previous lesson's balances. Start with $5,000 cash, supplied by the owners, and no other assets or liabilities. Cedar buys one batch of resale desks for $4,000 and sells the entire batch for $6,000.

Assume delivery transfers control, inventory is carried at cost, both parties pay on the stated dates, and there are no other transactions, fees, interest, taxes, or losses. This is a teaching timeline, not a complete real-company forecast.

DayEventCash movementCash balance
0Receive $4,000 inventory on supplier credit. Payment due day 20.$0$5,000
10Deliver the entire batch for $6,000 on customer credit. Payment due day 40.$0$5,000
20Pay the supplier's existing $4,000 bill.−$4,000$1,000
40Collect the customer's $6,000 invoice.+$6,000$7,000

Receiving inventory, earning revenue, paying a supplier, and collecting a customer are separate events. A cash timeline records receipts and payments on their dates, rather than substituting profit for cash. Formal reporting likewise distinguishes operating cash receipts/payments from profit. [1]

Where did the profit happen?

On day 10, Cedar recognizes $6,000 revenue when the customer obtains control. It also recognizes the $4,000 carrying cost of the sold inventory as an expense—cost of goods sold. Under our no-other-expenses assumption, profit is $2,000. [2] [3]

Cash is still $5,000 that day. Cedar has exchanged $4,000 inventory for a $6,000 customer receivable. Its $4,000 supplier obligation remains. Total assets are $11,000: $5,000 cash + $6,000 receivable; liabilities $4,000 + equity $7,000 gives the same total.

Paying the supplier on day 20 does not create a second $4,000 expense. Collecting the customer on day 40 does not create a second $6,000 sale. They settle the existing payable and receivable.

Immediately after the day-20 supplier payment, how much cash is available?

The timing gap: Cedar pays on day 20 but collects on day 40. It must fund that 20-day interval from other available cash or arranged funding. In the baseline, its $5,000 opening cash covers the $4,000 payment, leaving only $1,000 until collection.

Whose decision changes the timeline?

DepartmentDecision or activityConnection to cash
ProcurementNegotiate supplier price and payment termsAn earlier due date can pull cash out sooner, even if a discount looks attractive.
OperationsHold stock, fulfill orders, resolve delivery problemsDelays can push delivery and associated collection later, depending on contract terms.
SalesAgree customer price and payment termsLonger credit can push collection later, even without changing the sale's profit.
FinanceTrack due dates, collect invoices, forecast cashChecks whether the combined schedule can meet obligations; escalates funding gaps.

These are possible mechanisms, not guaranteed effects. Longer customer terms might help win business; longer supplier terms may cost more or be unavailable. Do not “solve” a cash gap by silently breaking an agreed payment deadline.

Agent-world rule: store the counterparty, amount, agreed due date, expected receipt/payment date, and actual settlement separately. A promise to pay is not cash. Test late-collection scenarios before approving commitments.

Your turn: introduce one competing obligation

Tell your teacher: Keep the timeline above, but now add $2,000 of wages for work performed in this cycle, payable on day 30. No other cash arrives before day 40, and no borrowing is available.
  1. How much cash is available immediately before the day-30 wages?
  2. What is the shortfall?
  3. Name one change to the plan that could prevent it, and whose agreement or action it would require.
Ask the agent anything unclear about the timeline, expense timing, or departmental dependencies.
