Cedar Desk Co. / Reference 03

Current ratio.

Current ratio = Current assets ÷ Current liabilities
$19,000 ÷ $6,000 ≈ 3.17:1
About $3.17 of recorded current assets per $1 of current liabilities.
TermQuick meaning
Current assetsCash and resources expected to turn into cash or be used up in the normal operating cycle or within a year. Examples under our assumptions: unrestricted cash, customer receivables, resale inventory.
Current liabilitiesShort-term obligations: for example, supplier bills and loan amounts due within twelve months. Not necessarily all the company's debt.
Non-currentOutside the current category. Examples: a machine used for years; loan principal that can be deferred for three years with no near-term installments.
Operating cycleThe cycle from acquiring inputs through selling and collecting customer payment. It is not always twelve months.
LiquidityThe ability to meet payment obligations as they fall due. Current ratio is one indicator, not a complete assessment.

These are introductory descriptions. Actual classification has more conditions; always check applicable reporting rules, restrictions on cash, and contractual payment terms.

Read the measure literally

  • At a date: numerator and denominator must come from the same snapshot.
  • Not just cash: inventory and receivables may not be immediately available for payment.
  • Not profit: it compares balance-sheet categories, not income and expenses.
  • Not a universal target: consider industry, seasonality, asset quality, and payment timing.
  • Zero denominator: if current liabilities are zero, division is undefined. Do not manufacture a numeric ratio or infer that all future cash needs disappear.

One transaction, two different signals

Cedar buys $10,000 of current inventory with cash: cash falls from $13,000 to $3,000; inventory rises from $6,000 to $16,000. Current assets stay $19,000 and current liabilities stay $6,000. The ratio stays 3.17:1, but immediately available cash is lower.

Agent-world check: pair ratio monitoring with a dated cash forecast, scheduled obligations, and checks on whether inventory can sell and receivables can be collected. Recompute aggregate totals after every transaction.

Sources: BDC's own explanations of current ratio, current assets, and current liabilities. Cedar's examples are simplified and fictional.
