Cedar Desk Co. / Reference 01

Three different questions.

MeasureMeaningTime basis
RevenueIncome from ordinary business activities. In our desk example, sales recognized when the customer obtains control of delivered desks—not merely when an order is signed or cash arrives.Over a period
ProfitIncome minus expenses for a period. In our simplified company, revenue is the only income, so profit = revenue − expenses. Always specify which profit measure; later lessons distinguish gross, operating, and net profit.Over a period
Cash balanceCash held at a particular moment. Our examples use only bank cash; formal reporting also considers cash equivalents.At a date
Net cash flowCash received minus cash paid over a period. Not the same thing as the closing cash balance.Over a period
ReceivableAn amount a customer owes us. In our example, delivered desks have been sold but not yet paid for.At a date
Two checks
Profit = revenue − expenses, in this simplified example.
Closing cash = opening cash + cash received − cash paid.

Agent-world rule: track sale, delivery, expense recognition, and payment separately. Attach a period or date to every reported measure. A revenue objective alone does not specify cash safety.

Sources & scope

IFRS 15 overview: revenue and transfer of control. IAS 7 overview: cash, cash flows, and profit-to-cash adjustments. Definitions here are introductory; the lesson uses explicit fictional assumptions, not a full accounting model.
