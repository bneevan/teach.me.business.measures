Day 02: Liquidity, Working Capital & The Dated Cash Calendar

Instructions: Solve the three problems below analyzing Cedar Desk Co.'s liquidity and payment schedule. When finished, paste your answers into the chat for review.

Key Concepts Tested: Current Ratio formula and composition limits (BDC), Liquidity vs. Solvency, Dated Cash Forecasts (IAS 7), 4-event operating timeline, Cash gap identification, and Cross-departmental coordination (Sales, Procurement, Operations, Finance).

Problem 2.1: The Current Ratio Illusion

On May 1, Cedar Desk Co. presents the following balance sheet balances:

  • Available Cash: $4,000
  • Raw Timber & Finished Desks (Inventory): $26,000
  • Customer Invoices (Accounts Receivable): $10,000 (all due after May 30)
  • Supplier Invoices (Current Liabilities): $10,000 (contractually due on May 15)

Questions to answer:

  1. Calculate Cedar's Current Assets and Current Ratio on May 1.
  2. A loan officer says: "Cedar has a healthy 4:1 current ratio, so paying the $10,000 bill on May 15 will be effortless." Is this true?
  3. If no customers pay and no inventory is liquidated before May 15, what will happen on May 15? What is Cedar's exact cash shortfall?

Problem 2.2: Building the Dated Cash Timeline

Cedar begins June with $8,000 in opening bank cash and no other transactions. The operations and sales schedule records five discrete events in June:

  • Day 5: Pay a supplier's existing bill: $5,000 cash outflow.
  • Day 10: Deliver $12,000 of desks to a customer on credit terms (customer payment contractually due on Day 35).
  • Day 18: Semi-monthly payroll and factory utility bills due: $6,000 cash outflow.
  • Day 25: Collection of an old overdue customer invoice: $2,500 cash inflow.
  • Day 30: Scheduled equipment maintenance payment: $1,500 cash outflow.

Questions to answer:

  1. Calculate Cedar's bank cash balance immediately after:
    • Day 5 payment
    • Day 10 delivery
    • Day 18 payroll/rent payment
    • Day 25 collection
    • Day 30 maintenance payment
  2. On which date does Cedar experience a cash deficit (insolvency), and how large is the shortfall on that day?
  3. Does recognizing the $12,000 desk sale on Day 10 prevent the cash shortfall on Day 18? Why or why not?

Problem 2.3: Cross-Departmental Action Plan

Cedar's management discovers the Day 18 cash shortfall before it happens.

Questions to answer:

  1. Why is it impermissible for a Treasury agent to "fix" the schedule simply by changing the payroll date in the software to Day 26?
  2. Propose two viable interventions involving other departments that could legally prevent the Day 18 shortfall, naming the specific department responsible for each:
    • Option 1 (Sales or Customer Credit action)
    • Option 2 (Procurement or Supplier action)
