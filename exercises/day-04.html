Day 04: The Income Statement Waterfall & Margins

Instructions: Solve the three problems below analyzing Cedar Desk Co.'s profitability across all 11 departments. When finished, paste your answers into the chat for review.

Key Concepts Tested: Gross Profit, Operating Profit (EBIT), Net Profit, Margin percentages, Departmental cost drivers across COGS and SG&A, Marketing breakeven formula (Cost ÷ Gross Margin %), and Percentage vs. Total Dollar optimization traps.

Problem 4.1: The Three-Tier Margin Waterfall

Cedar Desk Co. closes its monthly books with the following financial figures across its departments:

  • Total Customer Revenue: $150,000
  • Direct Manufacturing Costs (COGS):
    • Raw lumber and hardware (Procurement): $50,000
    • Direct factory labor & machine power (Operations): $32,500
  • Operating Overhead (SG&A):
    • Marketing campaigns & advertising: $18,000
    • Sales team commissions & client travel: $9,000
    • Customer service support staff: $4,000
    • IT software licenses & cloud infrastructure: $4,500
    • HR recruitment, training & payroll: $5,000
    • General admin, executive salaries & office rent: $8,500
    • Legal audits & compliance filings: $2,500
  • Financing & Taxes:
    • Bank loan interest expense: $2,500
    • Corporate income taxes: $3,500

Questions to answer:

  1. Calculate the total in dollars for:
    • Total Cost of Goods Sold (COGS)
    • Total Operating Expenses (SG&A)
    • Gross Profit
    • Operating Profit (EBIT)
    • Net Profit
  2. Calculate the three margin percentages:
    • Gross Margin %
    • Operating Margin %
    • Net Margin %

Problem 4.2: The Marketing Campaign Breakeven Test

Cedar's automated Marketing Agent proposes a $12,000 digital ad campaign to promote its new ergonomic executive desks.

Assume Cedar's Gross Margin remains identical to the percentage calculated in Problem 4.1 (and all other SG&A expenses remain fixed).

Questions to answer:

  1. Using the formula:
    Breakeven Revenue = Campaign Cost ÷ Gross Margin %
    How much new customer revenue must the campaign generate just to break even on Operating Profit?
  2. If the campaign generates $20,000 in new sales:
    • What is the incremental COGS needed to build those desks?
    • What is the incremental Gross Profit?
    • How much does Cedar's Operating Profit change in dollars (increase or decrease)?

Problem 4.3: Volume Discount vs. Margin Percentage

A regional hospital network wants to furnish their administrative offices and offers to buy 200 standing desks.

  • Standard retail price: $750 per desk
  • Direct production cost (COGS): $412.50 per desk (Standard Gross Margin = 45.0% or $337.50 gross profit per desk)
  • Hospital offer: They will buy all 200 desks immediately, but demand a 20% discount, offering to pay only $600 per desk. COGS remains $412.50. Cedar has ample idle factory capacity and incurs no extra operating overhead.

Questions to answer:

  1. At the discounted $600 price, what is Cedar's new Gross Margin % on each desk?
  2. In total dollars, how much Gross Profit does this single 200-desk order generate?
  3. If an autonomous Sales Agent is programmed solely to "never accept an order with a Gross Margin below 40%," what would it do? Why would that decision harm Cedar's cash and net profit?
