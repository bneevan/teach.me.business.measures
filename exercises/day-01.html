Day 01: Core Accounting, Cash Flow & The Balance Sheet

Instructions: Solve the four problems below based on Cedar Desk Co.'s first quarter. When finished, paste your answers into the chat for expert review and diagnostic feedback.

Key Concepts Tested: Revenue recognition upon transfer of control (IFRS 15), Operating cash collections vs. Profit (IAS 7), Inventory capitalization vs. Cost of Goods Sold (IAS 2), The Accounting Identity (Assets = Liabilities + Equity), and Loan financing mechanics.

Problem 1.1: Revenue Recognition vs. Cash Collection

In January, Cedar Desk Co. signs a $50,000 contract to supply 50 executive oak desks to Apex Corp ($1,000 per desk). Here is how the quarter plays out:

  • January 25: Cedar builds and delivers 30 desks ($30,000 value) to Apex's headquarters. Apex accepts delivery.
  • February 10: Cedar delivers the remaining 20 desks ($20,000 value). Apex accepts delivery.
  • Payment Schedule: Apex pays $15,000 cash in January upon the first delivery, $25,000 cash in February, and the final $10,000 cash balance in March.

Questions to answer:

  1. How much Revenue does Cedar recognize in January, February, and March?
  2. How much Cash does Cedar collect in January, February, and March?
  3. At the end of January (Jan 31), what is the balance of Cedar's Accounts Receivable from Apex?

Problem 1.2: Inventory Purchase vs. Expense (COGS)

On January 5, Cedar purchases $18,000 of raw timber on credit from a supplier (payment due in February). By January 31:

  • Cedar used $10,000 of that timber to manufacture the 30 desks delivered to Apex.
  • The remaining $8,000 of timber is sitting undamaged in Cedar's warehouse for future orders.
  • Cedar incurred no other production or operating costs in January.

Questions to answer:

  1. What is Cedar's Cost of Goods Sold (COGS) expense for January?
  2. On January 31, what is the carrying value of Cedar's Inventory asset on the balance sheet?
  3. Does buying $18,000 of timber on credit reduce Cedar's January profit by $18,000? Explain why or why not in 1–2 sentences.

Problem 1.3: Bank Loan Proceeds & The Accounting Identity

On January 1, Cedar started the year with $20,000 in Cash, $0 Liabilities, and $20,000 in Owner Equity.

On January 2, before making any sales or purchases, Cedar borrowed $15,000 cash from a local bank (a 3-year term loan). No interest has accrued yet.

Questions to answer:

  1. Immediately after the loan proceeds arrive on January 2, calculate Cedar's:
    • Total Assets
    • Total Liabilities
    • Total Equity
  2. Does receiving the $15,000 loan increase January revenue or profit? Why or why not?

Problem 1.4: AI Agent Design & Unintended Incentives

Suppose Cedar deploys an autonomous Sales Agent tasked with maximizing "Quarterly Recognized Performance." On January 10, right after Apex signs the $50,000 contract, the agent records the entire $50,000 as January Revenue in the ERP system.

Questions to answer:

  1. What core accounting rule (IFRS 15) does this agent action violate?
  2. If an autonomous Treasury Agent reads this premature $50,000 revenue entry and assumes cash will arrive in 15 days, what operational catastrophe could occur in Cedar's bank account?
  3. What explicit system guardrail should be coded into the Sales Agent's permissions?
