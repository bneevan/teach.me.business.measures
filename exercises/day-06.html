Day 06 Capstone: The Growth Paradox

Instructions: Solve the comprehensive four-part simulation below. It synthesizes revenue recognition, working capital, dated cash schedules, margins, and capital efficiency into a single realistic executive crisis. When finished, paste your answers into the chat for review.

The Setup: Cedar Desk Co. starts Q3 with $15,000 in bank cash, zero debt, and steady baseline operations that break even. Then, a massive enterprise client (TechCorp) awards Cedar an exclusive contract to furnish their new campus.

The TechCorp Deal Terms

  • Contract Price (Revenue): $120,000 for 120 custom height-adjustable desks ($1,000 each).
  • Manufacturing Timeline:
    • Day 0: Cedar orders $60,000 in raw hardwood & linear actuators on supplier credit (terms: due on Day 30).
    • Day 0: Cedar must purchase a specialized robotic edge-banding tool for $10,000 immediate cash (Fixed Asset / CapEx).
    • Day 45: Cedar finishes assembly and pays $20,000 in factory labor wages in cash.
    • Day 60: Cedar delivers all 120 desks to TechCorp. TechCorp accepts delivery and issues a formal acceptance certificate.
  • Customer Payment Terms: TechCorp is an enterprise giant with a rigid corporate policy: they pay invoices on Net 60 terms (payment contractually arrives 60 days after delivery, on Day 120).

Part 1: The Income Statement Waterfall (Lesson 06)

Analyze the profitability of this single contract assuming no other operating expenses:

  1. What is Cedar's Recognized Revenue, and on which exact day is it recognized? (Day 0, Day 45, Day 60, or Day 120?)
  2. What is Cedar's Cost of Goods Sold (COGS) for this contract (materials + direct labor)?
  3. Calculate Cedar's Gross Profit in dollars and Gross Margin %.
  4. Is this contract profitable on paper?

Part 2: Working Capital & The Cash Conversion Cycle (Lesson 05)

Analyze the operational time intervals for this batch:

  1. How many days does inventory sit in production from receipt (Day 0) to delivery (Day 60)? (DIO)
  2. How many days elapse between delivery (Day 60) and customer payment (Day 120)? (DSO)
  3. How many days elapse between material delivery (Day 0) and paying the supplier (Day 30)? (DPO)
  4. Calculate Cedar's Cash Conversion Cycle (CCC = DIO + DSO − DPO) for this contract.

Part 3: The Dated Cash Timeline & Insolvency Trough (Lessons 01, 03 & 04)

Trace Cedar's bank cash balance starting from $15,000 opening cash:

  1. Calculate Cedar's cash balance after:
    • Day 0: Pay $10,000 cash for the edge-banding machine → Cash = $__
    • Day 30: Pay $60,000 supplier bill → Cash = $__
    • Day 45: Pay $20,000 assembly labor → Cash = $__
    • Day 60: Deliver desks to TechCorp → Cash = $__
    • Day 120: Collect $120,000 from TechCorp → Cash = $__
  2. On which date does Cedar experience its worst cash trough, and what is the maximum cash deficit (shortfall)?
  3. Explain the "Growth Paradox": how can winning a highly profitable $120,000 contract bankrupt a growing company?

Part 4: The Multi-Agent Executive Solution (Lessons 04, 05, 07)

Cedar's executive leadership team gathers to prevent insolvency before signing the TechCorp contract. Propose a coordinated cross-departmental plan:

  1. Sales Agent Action: What negotiation could Sales have with TechCorp regarding the payment terms or milestones?
  2. Procurement Agent Action: What negotiation could Procurement have with the lumber/actuator supplier?
  3. Finance / Treasury Action: If external funding is needed to bridge the remaining gap, what financing instrument should Finance arrange, and what hurdle rate/interest cost must they monitor?
