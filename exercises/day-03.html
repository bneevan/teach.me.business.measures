Day 03: The Cash Conversion Cycle & Working Capital Velocity

Instructions: Solve the three problems below evaluating Cedar Desk Co.'s working capital efficiency and supplier terms. When finished, paste your answers into the chat for review.

Key Concepts Tested: DIO, DSO, DPO, Operating Cycle, Cash Conversion Cycle (CCC = DIO + DSO − DPO), Working Capital financing gaps, Trade credit discounts ("2/10 net 40"), Annualized Rate of Return (APR), and Departmental trade-offs.

Problem 3.1: Calculating Working Capital Metrics

Cedar Desk Co. operates with the following supply chain timeline:

  • Raw timber and components sit in inventory for an average of 45 days before being assembled and shipped (DIO = 45 days).
  • Corporate clients take an average of 50 days to pay invoices after delivery (DSO = 50 days).
  • Lumber suppliers require payment within 30 days of delivery (DPO = 30 days).

Questions to answer:

  1. Calculate Cedar's Operating Cycle in days.
  2. Calculate Cedar's Cash Conversion Cycle (CCC) in days.
  3. Explain in plain language what Cedar's CCC number means: during this interval, where is Cedar's money, and how must operations be funded?

Problem 3.2: Departmental Levers & Trade-Offs

Cedar's executive team mandates a working capital overhaul to cut the Cash Conversion Cycle in half:

  • Procurement initiative: Unilaterally delay supplier payments from 30 days to 60 days (DPO = 60).
  • Sales initiative: Demand that all business clients pay within 15 days instead of 50 days (DSO = 15).
  • Operations initiative: Cut warehouse inventory buffers so stock is held for only 10 days before assembly (DIO = 10).

Questions to answer:

  1. If all three targets are magically achieved, what would Cedar's new CCC be? Is this a positive or negative cycle?

Problem 3.3: Evaluating a Trade Credit Prompt Discount

Cedar receives a $20,000 raw material invoice from a key lumber mill with terms: "2/10 net 40".

This means Cedar can deduct 2% if it pays on Day 10; otherwise, the full $20,000 is due on Day 40.

Questions to answer:

  1. Calculate:
    • Dollar discount saved if paid on Day 10: $__
    • Actual cash paid out on Day 10: $__
    • Number of days payment is pulled forward: __ days
  2. Calculate the raw period return (Dollar discount ÷ Cash paid) and the Simple Annualized Return (APR):
    APR = Period Return × (365 ÷ Days accelerated)
  3. Cedar's bank offers a revolving line of credit at an 8% annual interest rate. If Cedar has available credit, does it make economic sense to borrow from the bank line to capture the prompt-payment discount? Explain why.
